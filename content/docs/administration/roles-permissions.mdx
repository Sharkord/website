Permissions come from roles. A user has one or more roles, and their permissions are the union of what those roles grant. There is no "deny" at the server level: a permission is granted by any role, or it is not granted at all.

## The Two Built-in Roles

A fresh server has:

- **Owner**, which holds every permission and bypasses every check, including private channels. It cannot be deleted, and its permissions cannot be edited. You get it by claiming the [access token](/docs/introduction/owner-permissions).
- **Member**, the default role. New users are given it automatically, and it starts with: send messages, upload files, join voice channels, share screen, enable webcam.

Any role can be made the default from the Roles tab. Deleting a role moves everyone who had it to the default role.

Managing these needs `MANAGE_ROLES`.

### Member Permissions

| Permission          | Grants                                               |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Send messages       | Sending messages in channels, and typing indicators. |

## Channel Permissions

Six permissions can be set per channel: **View channel**, **Send messages**, **Join channel**, **Speak**, **Share screen**, and **Enable webcam**. Editing them needs `MANAGE_CHANNEL_PERMISSIONS`.

They can be granted to a role or to a single user.

### How They Resolve

The rules, in order:

1. **Public channels grant everything to everyone.** Overrides on a public channel do nothing. To restrict a channel, make it private.
2. **The owner always passes**, on any channel.
3. **View channel gates the rest.** A user who cannot see a channel cannot do anything in it, whatever else is granted.
4. **A user-level entry wins**, whether it grants or denies. This is the only way to deny something.
5. **Otherwise, any role granting it wins.** Role entries are additive.
6. **Otherwise it is denied.** Private channels start closed.

So a private channel is closed until you grant view to somebody, and one user can be excluded from a channel their role otherwise opens.

### Voice Channels

Joining a voice channel needs the server-level **Join voice channels** permission plus the channel-level **Join**.

Publishing is checked twice. The channel permission is always required (**Speak** for the microphone, **Enable webcam** for video, **Share screen** for screen sharing and its audio), and video and screen sharing additionally require the matching server-level permission. So removing **Share screen** from a role stops that role sharing anywhere, public channels included, while a channel-level override restricts it to one channel.

Losing one of these while connected takes the corresponding stream away rather than disconnecting you.

Moving someone else between voice channels needs **Move members**, and the target has to be allowed to join the destination.

## Per-Role Storage Quotas

A role can carry a storage quota that overrides the server-wide per-user quota. Enable the override on the role and set the amount.

When a user has several roles with overrides, the largest quota wins, and a role whose quota is `0` means unlimited, which beats everything. Without any override role, the global per-user quota applies. See [Storage and Uploads](./storage).
